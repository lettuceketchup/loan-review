Finding the sweet spot

What this means for you: Pick a loan to see how much extra to pay.

Paying extra always saves interest. Every extra rupee earns the loan's rate, and it is guaranteed. The sweet spot is where paying more stops being the best use of your money, because of your cash limits or what the money could earn elsewhere.

Prepay, raise the EMI or lower it

What this means for you: Paying extra early saves the most. A ₹1 L lump sum paid now saves about {{lump1_save|lakh}} on the Home Loan. Lowering the EMI makes the loan cost about {{lowemi_cost|lakh}} more in interest, so do it only if you must.

The table under "More detail" changes one thing on the Home Loan at a time and compares it with carrying on as now (EMI {{2916_emi|inr}}, rate {{2916_rate|r2}}%, no extras). Suraksha is covered in the calculators further down.

Early money works hardest

Interest is charged on the balance every day. A rupee paid now stops costing about {{2916_rate|r2}}% a year for the rest of the loan. A ₹1 L lump sum paid now saves about {{lump1_save|lakh}}. The same ₹1 L paid a year later saves about {{lump1d_save|lakh}}. Waiting costs less than most people expect, so pay a lump sum when the cash is really spare.

Raising the EMI and paying extra come to the same thing

₹10,000 extra a month and an EMI raised by ₹10,000 give the same result. The difference is flexibility. You can skip an extra payment in a tight month, but you cannot skip a higher EMI. An EMI that rises 5% each year from the second year ends the loan in {{step5_months}} months, and costs nothing extra in the first year.

Lowering the EMI costs a lot

To match the bank's {{2916_bankRemaining}} months, the EMI would be about {{neutral_emi|inr}}. That costs about {{lowemi_cost|lakh}} more interest than today's EMI. It can help in a hard year, and you can raise the EMI again later.

A rate change moves the end date

The EMI stays fixed, so a higher rate makes the loan longer and a lower rate makes it shorter. +0.5 points adds about {{rup_months}} months. −0.5 points takes off about {{rdn_months}}. To keep the same end date when the rate rises, the EMI has to go up (use calculator 3).

Which loan first

Prepaying the Home Loan ({{2916_rate|r2}}%) saves a little more per rupee than Suraksha ({{1105_rate|r2}}%), and far more in total, because the Home Loan stays unpaid much longer. {{sura_bal|lakh}} paid into the Home Loan saves about {{sura_on_home|lakh}}. The same amount paid into Suraksha saves all of its remaining {{sura_int|lakh}} of interest. If Suraksha is closed and its EMI is added to the Home Loan EMI, total interest ends within {{roll_gap|inr}} of putting the lump sum on the Home Loan. So the choice is about keeping things simple, not about savings.

What is not in these numbers

Tax relief on interest (Section 24(b)) and on principal (Section 80C) can change the real cost. If your yearly interest is already above the tax limit, the interest you save does not lower your tax, so compare using the loan's full rate. Check with your CA. The statements show no charge for prepaying. Loans with a floating rate to individuals usually have none, but ask the branch before a large payment.

How the numbers were checked

What this means for you: The figures were checked four ways against the bank's own statements. You can leave this section closed unless you want to see the proof.

The numbers come from the bank's yearly Excel files (to March 2025), the password-protected PDFs for the two loans, and Excel statements for the savings account. They were joined into {{n_rows}} rows and tested four ways.

More detail: the four checks

Every balance follows from the one before it

Each row's balance equals the previous balance, less debits and plus credits. This holds across every file. Rows on the same day were put in the order that makes the balances chain.

Gaps between statements were found and filled

{{n_derived}} items were missing from the bank's own statements, because each old statement stopped before the month's last postings. They are shown as "derived" rows. Each one is the exact amount that makes the balances chain close. They are listed below.

The bank's interest was reproduced

Interest worked out again from daily balances and the listed rates matches the bank's figure in all {{n_recon}} loan-months checked from March 2025 onward, to within ₹{{max_diff|r2}}.

Savings and loans agree

From April 2025, every payment into a loan was matched to a debit in the savings account (same amount, within three days). Every loan-bound debit in the savings account was matched to a loan credit. {{unmatched_text}}
